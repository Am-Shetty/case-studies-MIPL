How the programme began

Chosen among 100. Built around one control room.

Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (formerly Aurangabad) was selected in 2018 under the Government of India's Smart City Mission — one of 100 cities nationally picked to pilot area-based and pan-city digital transformation. Aurangabad Smart City Development Corporation Limited (ASCDCL) was set up to lead that mandate, and one of its first and most consequential decisions was the design of an Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC): a single platform and video wall bringing together the city's own Operations Control Centre (OCC) and the police's Command and Control Centre (CCC), so that traffic, surveillance, utilities and emergency response could be seen and coordinated from one room.

MIPL has served as ASCDCL's Project Management Consultant since 2019, carrying that founding design through seven years of execution. What has followed is a portfolio of more than 30 digital initiatives, organised on ASCDCL's own project mind map across six domains — Governance (7 projects), Citizen Centric services (11), Mobility (3), Education (2), Security (5) and cross-cutting additional initiatives (2), with 3 more in planning.

The city's challenge

Make separate systems work as one city.

A municipal service rarely begins and ends in one department. A property record affects taxation, permissions and doorstep services. A vehicle location matters to both a transport operator and a waiting passenger. A school attendance record can trigger support for a child at risk of dropping out.

For ASCDCL, Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar Municipal Corporation (CSMC) and the Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar City Police, the challenge was to connect these everyday needs with reliable information, clear workflows and operational oversight across a wide-ranging digital programme, without 30+ initiatives becoming 30+ disconnected systems.

MIPL's contribution

Consulting continuity across a complex programme.

As PMC since 2019, MIPL has assisted ASCDCL and CSMC in conceptualising and executing IT initiatives and ensuring deliverables align with design. The programme's outcomes belong to a collaborative effort involving the city, police, municipal departments and implementation partners.

The wider impact

From digital infrastructure to everyday public value.

For citizens: more ways to access services, clearer information, simpler payments, and a 42% rise in bus ridership as digital ticketing replaced queues and uncertainty.

For the city: ₹180 Cr+ in property tax collected with a rising digital share, shared spatial records, visible field operations and better coordination across revenue, utilities, schools and transport.

For public safety: No. 1–ranked ICCC infrastructure, evidence-led policing backed by 500+ CCTV-solved cases, ₹40 Cr+ in enforced traffic discipline, and a documented foundation for strengthening cybersecurity.

Together, these outcomes show how sustained programme consulting can help a city connect technology investment with the way people live and public institutions work.
